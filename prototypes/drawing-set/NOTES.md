# The Drawing Set

## Philosophy
Kettle is drawn the way an architect documents a building. Each domain is a **room**, each capability a **bay** inside it, each feature a **fixture**. The six lenses are the **sheets** of the set (G-001 general arrangement, B-101 business, D-201 design, S-301 structure, M-401 services, F-501 fire & life safety, Q-601 inspection). Switching sheet changes what the places say, never where they are. Trouble is a red **revision cloud**, a watch item is an amber tick, a recent change is a revision **delta**. The **title block** holds the schedule, and the **revisions strip** holds time. Plain words come first (Built, Partly open, Construction, Proposed). The evidence sits underneath, on the feature's own sheet.

## How it reads at 132 and at 528
It works like a map, with levels: **L0 site → L1 wing → L2 room → L3 bay → L4 feature sheet** (at `?scale=4`, a campus level and a building level sit above the wings). Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, and click to go one level deeper. A level's labels appear only once they can be drawn at a readable size. Until then the level shows as shape and colour: stage fills, clouds, deltas, aggregate bars. Nothing on the map is drawn below 12 px, and nothing is cut mid-word. If a name does not fit, it waits for the next zoom step.

## Moments
- **Serves best:** 1 the glance (L0 shows five labelled wings with live, building and trouble counts, plus the schedule), 2 lenses (seven sheets, places fixed), 3 one feature (a full document sheet), 5 find (`/`, Enter flies there and opens it), 6 what changed (deltas plus the 24h/7d/14d window and the revisions strip; time travel regrows the building), 8 Payments GA (`G`), 9 showing outward.
- **Serves less well:** 7 blast radius under stress. `B` washes the dependents red and lists them, but you have to pick the source feature first. 4 the projector review works best at L1–L2. At L0 the feature fixtures are texture only.
- Phones get a reading list grouped by wing and room. They do not get the zoomable plan.

## What changed in round 2
- **Continuous semantic zoom on a canvas renderer.** Wheel, pinch, drag, +/− keys and fly-to transitions run through site → wing → room → bay → feature sheet. *Answers: "L0 will need figma/google map approach … and expand nested layers on scroll to there."*
- **A true L0 above the floor plan: five wings** (Foundations, Studio operations, Money, Members & reach, Frontier). Each wing is labelled at display size with an aggregate line, a stage bar and a trouble count, and carries no feature tags. *Answers: "display abstract high level sections on first sight."*
- **Labels gated by on-screen size, with a 12 px floor everywhere.** Capability tags appear once the bay header can hold them. Feature names appear once a fixture is about 100 px wide, and evidence and tensions appear at about 150 px. At overview there are no more truncated ids. DOM and canvas text were both checked: minimum 12 px, body 14 px. *Answers: "designs are then forced to overhaul UI with small text … realistically unreadable content."*
- **Left-to-right chronological structure.** Wings are ordered by the median start date of their work, computed from the data. Inside every bay the features run oldest to newest, so time travel regrows the plan from the left. A ruler under the plan says so, and the intro draws the building left to right. *Answers: "operate canvas in chronological structure from left to right instead of top to bottom."*
- **`?scale=4` campus.** The data is cloned in memory into Kettle Studios, Gyms, Pools and Clubs, with ids suffixed: 52 rooms and 528 features. L0 then shows four buildings with aggregates only. Wing and room labels hold back until there is room for them. *Answers: "Otherwise the UI cannot scale much more and keep clarity."*
- **Algorithmic floor plan with a stepped silhouette.** Rooms are no longer stretched or hand-placed. Each wing picks its column count so the building balances, and the outline steps where the wings end. This is what lets the same drawing hold at any size.
- **Where am I, at every level:** a breadcrumb with the current place's counts, a level ladder (L0–L4, clickable), a key plan (minimap, clickable), a scale bar showing "one feature", and Esc to go up one level. *Answers the host's "A breadcrumb or minimap always says where you are and how to get back."*
- **Tensions in words instead of numbered hexes.** Fixtures carry "Agent-built, unreviewed", "No alerting" and so on. The panel lists the nine tensions with counts; clicking one dims everything else. *Answers the round-1 defect "numbered tension badges need the keynote legend to decode."*
- **Hovered item at centre stage:** a large hover card carries the name at display size, the stage in plain words, the current sheet's evidence and the tensions. For wings and rooms it adds that domain's stylised artwork.
- **The feature sheet as a document:** a full-height surface with six lens panels in two readable columns (the current sheet highlighted), tension chips, dependencies, red-pencil notes, revision history and activity. The map keeps the feature in view beside it.
- **Kept from round 1:** the drawing-set language, the sheets as lenses, revision clouds, the title block, the room schedule, the revisions strip, Payments GA and blast radius modes, the vellum sweep on sheet change, and the per-domain line art. All of it is labelled stylised sample data.
